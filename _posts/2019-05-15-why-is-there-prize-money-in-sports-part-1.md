Jojoba was ranked three meals higher than her opponent Mustard. Mustard was strong and athletic, or at least he had been until he started playing as a professional. Even then, he had sturdy wrists that could generate a lot of side spin. Jojoba had delicate wrists but her placement was excellent. Her paddle was also crafted for control. Even with fewer revs on the ball, her service was tricky to return since she could hit the two extremities of the table with the same service motion.

Jojoba went to Mustard's forehand. It was the right choice because the numbers suggested that his forehand was weaker. She could not risk not playing the percentages at this stage of the match. Mustard had lost a few close matches of late and that made him wary, even on match point, especially on match point. He just put the ball back in play. Jojoba considered going for it but the ball wasn't short enough and the angle also made it tricky to hit a high percentage winner. So she chopped it back. Mustard did the same and thus started another slugfest. The audience booed. Nikuma expressed her displeasure by throwing her beer bottle at some unsuspecting spectator in the crowd. It was later found out that the said spectator was executed for not being able to catch the said projectile as he was deemed "unsportworthy".

Nikuma, the sovereign ruler of Nikuma, was a patron of the best Nikuma players in the world. This was hardly surprising since the sport Nikuma was invented by Nikuma. The people of Nikuma referred to her as "Sportsperson Supreme" and the official records maintained that she "was an epitome of the ideal sportsperson and the greatest player, apart from God herself, of any sport ever conceived", including Nikuma, which was the "greatest sport, apart from life itself, ever conceived".

Jojoba was among these elite Nikuma players who enjoyed Nikuma's patronage. Eventually, Mustard got lucky as one of his shots caught the net and landed on the right side. Jojoba was left to rue her chances. Her coach, Olive, insinuated that she wasn't brave enough to go for the clean winner. "Easy for you to say when starvation isn't on the line for you," she retorted.

"Easy for me? I survived on the circuit for ten years, kid. In fact, you have it easy. For the first couple of my pro years, you didn't just starve for a day, you were executed for losing."

"Yeah, I have heard the stories. I think you are full of shit though. How do you not lose a match for two years? You people probably just made these stories up."

"Do not trivialize our sacrifices, kid. I did not lose a match for two years because I competed like there was no tomorrow, which, quite conveniently, there wasn't if you lost."

"Sacrifices? Yeah, right. It wasn't like you had a choice. They just choose us randomly from a crowd of hundreds. Also, are you telling me you had a five hundred match winning streak or something? We play here every day and none of us has a five hundred match winning streak. It's impossible. And you aren't even that good. I have beaten you a few times in practice."

Olive sighed. The few players alive from her generation were generally treated with respect, almost awe. They were the ultimate survivors. But the girl wasn't completely wrong. "Look, things were different back then. Nikuma was a new sport. It was supposed to be the ultimate test of poise, grace, guile, agility and skill. But apart from the Sportsperson Supreme, none of us had completely mastered the variety of spins. The paddles were not that great, and really, it was a coin toss as to who won initially. The new players would almost certainly lose against the more experienced ones like us who had managed to survive our first few matches. Sportsperson Supreme didn't pit us against each other until very late and since the pool of players constantly kept getting halved, I might have only played fifty matches over that two-year period, with only five or ten against players who had a little bit of experience."

Jojoba was slightly taken aback. None of the other coaches had been honest with her about their own pro days. It was just endless showboating. They hated the fact that players these days were given glucose shots just before the match. It was a disgrace, they had declared. The coaching she received was mostly useless advice about being in that zone where you needed to spend minimal energy getting to balls because you can see everything in slow motion. She did not believe any of it was possible for a human, except for Nikuma, of course.

"Why did they stop executing players?" she asked.

"As I said, too many players were getting killed off. It wasn't sustainable. It wasn't helping the sport. And the Sportsperson Supreme is not cruel. She only wanted to see people achieve their full potential like she had done. However, she eventually realized that we cannot do what she does. So she tried to motivate people in a different way. She started executing the families of the players who had lost. The people who had not lost yet had an incentive to win every time they went out in the arena and those who had already lost would know no fear. Unfortunately, this experiment also did not work and she had to shelve it within a month. The players who lost would go into depression. Those who were forced to play after that and were compassionate gave their opponents easy wins."

"What? Seriously? How come I have never heard of this?"

"The experiment was limited in scope. Not all of us were part of it. And from among those who were part of it, I was the only one who made it out alive. The others committed suicide or did something stupid and were executed."

"Woah… how… how did you get through it?"

"Oh, it was easy for me. I had no family."
