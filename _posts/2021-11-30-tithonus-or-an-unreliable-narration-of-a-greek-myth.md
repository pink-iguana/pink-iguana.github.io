**Tithonus**

Aurora!
Goddess of dawn, Aurora!
A queen in flight, Aurora!
Herald of Helios, Aurora!
Rose-fingered, Aurora!
Golden-throned, Aurora!
Saffron-robed, Aurora!
Cursed by love, Aurora!
Once my lover, Aurora…
Au… ro… ra…
Au-ror-ror-ror-

Once…
What a word! Once a word!
Maybe even a sentence,

Like molten cast iron poured haphazardly into misshapen moulds
Oh! The giddiness I felt when she stole me from my bed
Parting my dark locks of youth to peer at this stupid face
With her ancient child-like bored hungry eyes
We played the same silly love games over and over
As she replenished her rose, gold, and saffron every day;
But I changed a little each time she splintered the night.

Must change my nature of changing!
We could live together forever unchanging!
A mortal, an immortal and their unholy changelings!
She ran giggling to Zeus, chuffed and uncalculating,
The shifty bastard was red with envy:
Why would she love a human and not him?
Why would anyone love anyone that wasn't him?
How can he stick anything, somewhere, anywhere devoid of him?
A hole, he did find in his grant of immortality
Wither but never die, ruin but do not perish
Become the chaff that outlives the corn
Here, beyond the clutch of humanity,
I am slowly turning into a grotesque sculpture
That even a goddess cannot love.

She won't look at me anymore
Weak, meek, decrepit, and sick,
While I can only peek at her
Sitting astride her new horses
And riding them all day long,
Their burnished unblemished coats
Reflecting her dazzling radiance
Hello! I am also parked in the garage,
Although my leather is wrinkly and worn,
Now untouched by dusk or dawn.
Babbity-babble-rabbity-rabble!
A few cicadas teach me their song
I know not why but before long,
I might be joining their throng.

**Zeus**

I did not lie, Aurora,
The love of mortals is fickle
And so must their bodies be
When spring colours the meadows,
They swear undying love
But as autumn takes its turn
Their devotion sheds its wings
I heard your prayer for Tithonus
Yet even I cannot unmake him
Overcome by an infatuation too potent,
He thought himself above his kin,
But immortality does not befit, a modest mind as his.

Did I not warn you, Aurora?
He will resent you and I,
They are always ungrateful,
Their entire kind.
This was known to me all along
Yet I could not have turned you away
The way you stirred such passions within me,
Passions, to be certain, of generosity.
I bestowed upon him what I could,
Vexed by a suspicion to have already gone too far.

I cannot annul my gift, Aurora
They will think me a miser
Nor can I grant eternal youth
Lest I invite the fury of the Fates
Ignorant fool, Tithonus,
I pity him, as much as you do,
If not for these divine rules that bind me
I would restore him
If only to dispel his unjust reproach for you.

What I have is counsel,
Cleitus and Tithonus,
Orion and Cephalus,
They will all come and go
And while I may shield you from Aphrodite's curse
Your heart will suffer a thousand griefs more
So come here, Aurora,
Come quick, before Hera appears,
I will mend this one for you,
They say cicadas love you as much as other mortals do.

**Aurora**

I will stop the days
If the days don't dawn, there will be no days
Every day I dawn,
My sweet charming Tithonus slips further away
Every dawn I am reborn,
And that perfect day fades a little more
Oh! He was so taken by surprise
The first time he woke up in my arms
So young, so supple, so naïve
Rubbing rheumy eyes and pupils so wide;
He clung to me as if my horses would devour him!
Oh! How do I drink that morning dew again?
Or feel the gentle sun on our naked backs,
As the blooming roses smeared their scent in his messy hair.

Sometimes we played hide and seek
I would always find him
Hiding from me in dark corners like a cute little rat,
Sometimes I would let him chase me
Around the halls, frantic and sweaty,
Until he gave up or I let him win.
All this while, Nyx worked her dark magic
Stripping him of his boyhood innocence
Layering him with mirthless calluses that men often wear.
She took my lovely tiny Tithonus
And morphed him into my grim old lover, Ares.

I had to do something!
They would have stolen my Tithonus!
When I heard Aphrodite cackling in delight,
I ran to the mighty Zeus,
They say he is just and wise,
Pleaded with him to have my Tithonus back
To never have him swindled from me.
Zeus, unsmiling, brooding, and dire,
Said it was too great an ask,
How could mortals desire immortality?
I said, "I want him. I want him. I want him forever!"
He said: "I see that you fancy this mortal much, however – "
"Actually, never mind, this should work very well."
And it did! It worked for a moment,
My carefree Tithonus breathed again,
Once more we played our love games,
Him taming my unruly curls while I mussed his hair
Or busted his hiding dens,
But when he ran ragged after me,
He gave up more easily each time,
Until one day he collapsed and sighed,
"I can't keep up anymore."
I pressed his frail body against my frame,
Cradled him in my arms and reassured him:
"There will be brighter days ahead."
He smiled wistfully and said:
"Indeed, Aurora, they fill me with dread."
What did he mean? How could he say that?
Did he crave darkness? Was I too much for him?
How could I have known? What could I have done?
Why would he not just be the boy I had known?
Cleitus, Orion, Cephalus, Tithonus,
Why must this always happen to me?

These days, he rambles his days away
Or maybe that's how cicadas speak
He wanes in the absence of my light,
But maybe that's what cicadas want
If he was a cicada, would he be happy?
If he was a cicada, would he still love me?
Maybe it would be a mercy, as Zeus tells me.
Having lost his human mind to ravages of immortality,
You might say he is half a cicada already,
If so, it could only be a mercy.
