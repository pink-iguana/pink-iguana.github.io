*This piece is not a work of philosophy, not a parody of Marx's essay on Commodity Fetishism, and definitely not a polemic against dogs. Cats are essentially simple creatures. They forage, sleep, reproduce and generally go about their business as animals do. As long as they are viewed as selfish creatures engaged in material processes, there is no magic of cats. Then what is it that gives them their nine lives, their phantom-like quality of being zen masters and their immense fan base on the internet? What is it that lets them grow out of their brain-to-body mass ratio and become the transcendental creatures that dominate the web on a scale similar to pornography? Why is Pusheen the loveable fat tabby that is so cute that it makes you drool rather than the grotesque cover of a polemic against consumerism?

To understand the magic of cats, or cat fetishism, one has to look at the abstraction involved in the depiction of animals wherein they are reduced to caricatures with certain traits rather than the animals that they are. It is then that the behaviour of a species is mapped as qualities of a depiction of an individual of that species and then viewed against human traits as if the animal had a human personality. This is the classic case of anthropomorphism. With this, as mentioned earlier, certain properties are also abstracted out to simplify the depiction (however with great distortion) of the animal to make it accessible and attractive for the general surfer on the internet. Such a distortion is widely seen in other mediums too, fables being an important example. In the particular case, the solitary nature of cats and their stealth are abstracted out to portray cats as ninjas or the general propensity of cats to be laid back casts them as gurus enamoured of mystical knowledge. In this context, the deification of cats comes to mind, Bast being an obvious example.

Cats have been admired for their poise, cleanliness, and grace across several cultures. However, if these are the sole reasons for the apotheosis of cats on the internet, one may as well argue that snakes should be considered divine unanimously. But snakes are reptiles and humans as mammals have an ingrained mistrust of them. In fact, cat fetishism has one more important aspect. Domestic cats, although not wild, have retained an air of mystery. While other domesticated animals have become too much unlike their wilder cousins (for instance, compare dogs and wolves), cats have not become the use-me-when-upset creatures tailor-made to suck up to humans. In other words, cats are thought of as having personality from a completely anthropomorphic viewpoint. Since domestic animals are the most common points of contact with other animals for humans, cats are conspicuous enough to be seen in their full glory.

Suppose we were to abstract out the qualities of a domestic dog and look at them as human qualities. One would be hard pressed to find a desirable quality. For instance, lack of cleanliness would look very bad on a human resume. The amount of noise that dogs make settling their personal feuds is also clearly a problem. Dog-like loyalty, though useful, often has a servile connotation.

If a dog-person finds the preceding paragraph as insinuations of a cat-bigot, so be it. It's a rationalisation that every dog-person deserves. But in case the wound of insult is still sore, one could take recourse to the cat-way. The cat-way (alternatively known as the zen way) allows us to enter the mist-enveloped regions of spirituality which binds all the fetishism related to the realm of cats. There are usually two ways to carry out a task at hand, namely the cat-way and not the cat-way. The former involves minimum effort and maximum grace and the latter can be all sorts of brutish combinations. This cat-way is the ultimate abstraction in all cat depictions. It is what grants cats their cuteness and allows us to view Pusheen's life as a perfectly good model of life. Our tolerance of the havoc created by Simon's cat is the embodiment of an inner desire to adhere to the cat-way.

The utility of cats is universally recognised. The furry creatures cure humans of depression, get rid of vermin, give important life advice to mythical heroes and greet kids endowed with an overactive imagination (or adults with excessive intake of hallucinogens) with creepy smiles. But the real fetishism arises from the exchange of cat stickers on Facebook. It is then that Pusheen stickers are compared with other available stickers in the Sticker Store (like Tuzki and More Tuzki) and their cuteness, an abstract quality, is quantified. Suddenly cats start competing for server space with minions without themselves having any desire to do so.

Cat fetishism is not a model limited to cats. Similar principles are afoot in people's aversion of hyenas after *The Lion King* or their acceptance of the majesty of a lion after again, *The Lion King* or their belief in the concept of evil lions after the same movie or their ideas about romance between lions or their belief that herbivores can like lions after the Disney classic.

So next time when you see someone force-feeding carrots to rabbits or for that matter lasagne to an orange fat tabby, you know you have seen cat fetishism in action.
